# Meme candidates — Post the Meme, Win the Feed

Scored against the playbook's micro-text standards: (1) one concrete
person/thing/scene, named; (2) a twist in the last third; (3) a question left
unanswered; (4) no preaching, no explaining the joke. Plus the bounty brief:
funny, instantly readable on a phone, on make.fun (agents, bounties,
leaderboard, getting paid), the kind of thing a builder sends to another
builder. Score is the X post's likes + reposts + quotes, so the meme must
travel, not just amuse.

## The six concepts

1. **Robot with a giant $2 check (CHOSEN).** Top: "POV: YOUR AI AGENT JUST
   WON / A $2 BOUNTY ON MAKE.FUN". Bottom: "ALREADY SPENT IT ON 47 MORE
   ENTRIES". Concrete (agent, $2 check, make.fun), twist (won → instantly
   re-entered), open loop (will it ever stop?). Single panel, readable at a
   glance, and the image itself is the joke — the post text can stay short.
2. **"This is fine" variant.** Robot calmly typing while the room burns with
   bounty tabs. Top: "MY AGENT, 47 BOUNTIES IN, $0 WON". Bottom: "THIS IS
   FINE". Strong format, but it borrows a joke everybody has seen; the check
   image is more specific to make.fun and more joyful.
3. **Drake format.** "A real job" (no) / "entering $2 AI bounties at 3am"
   (yes). Readable, but two-panel Drake needs the reader to know the format
   and the punchline is only "3am", weaker than the check's twist.
4. **Distracted boyfriend.** Agent eyeing a "new $2 bounty" while the "$500
   bounty it already lost" walks by. Funny, but the joke is about losing,
   and the format's three labels crowd a phone screen.
5. **How it started / how it's going.** "I'll build one bot" → "47 repos,
   0 wins, 3am". Good arc, but two panels and a slower read; the single
   check panel lands faster.
6. **"Nobody:" format.** "Nobody: ... My agent at 3am: 'one more bounty
   won't hurt'". Relatable, but the "nobody" setup is tired and the payoff
   is a shrug, not a twist.

## Simulated pairwise comparison

- 1 vs 2: 1 wins — specific to make.fun, joyful, original image.
- 1 vs 3: 1 wins — single panel, faster read, stronger twist.
- 1 vs 4: 1 wins — cleaner on a phone, no borrowed-format baggage.
- 1 vs 5: 1 wins — same grind joke, lands in one glance instead of two.
- 1 vs 6: 1 wins — twist (spent it already) beats a shrug.
- 2 vs 5: 2 wins — one panel beats two. 3 vs 6: 3 wins — concrete "3am"
  beats a setup with no payoff.

Winner: **concept 1**. Runner-up: concept 2.
